## 1. Questions
- **RQ1 Reporting.** For the 45 action-taking clinical agent benchmarks released 2024-01-01 to 2026-10-03, what share report each agent-specific property (A1-A11) and each reused core property (C1-C14)?
- **RQ2 Can the audit be automated reliably?** How far do independent coders from three model families agree when they score identical evidence (Krippendorff's alpha; every pair is cross-family under v3, so the pairwise values are primary and the all-three value is secondary)? How stable is each coder under repetition (test-retest)? How well does the full pipeline recover planted text and detect change under perturbation?
- **RQ3 Executed behaviour (existence demonstration).** On 3 runnable benchmarks not executed by papers 20 or 21 (AgentClinic, RadA-BenchPlat, Synthetic Hospital), with one small local agent, how much do agent actions and grader verdicts vary across 5 identical reruns, how wide are headline-score intervals at 1 run versus 5, and, where state is observable, how often does the grader disagree with the end state? RQ3 is framed as end-to-end variability of the benchmark as an instrument (agent, simulator and judge together), not as a property of benchmarks in general. A judge-only rerun separates the judge for AgentClinic.
- Exploratory, labelled as such: reporting by interface type and release year.

The search ran 2026-10-03 over arXiv, PubMed, the ACL Anthology and backward chaining. 7,233 unique records; 186 assessed in full text; 45 included. ## 3. Instrument
Item list in `rubric/rubric-v1.md`; anchors and global rules G1-G7 in `rubric/coding-manual-v1.0.md`. Anchors were developed in two pilot rounds on five benchmarks; agreement between two Sonnet coders (same family) was 63/75, then 46/50. These are development figures, not cross-family agreement. ## 4. Automated audit (`scorer/ARCHITECTURE.md`)
Steps (architecture v3): evidence packets at pinned versions, capped at 150k tokens by a deterministic S4 truncation rule; whole-packet scoring with no retrieval step, so every coder reads the identical packet. Coders from three families score all 25 items per benchmark in one call (or fixed item groups): Anthropic claude-sonnet-5-5 (Claude CLI, tools disabled), OpenAI via Codex CLI (read-only sandbox, run from an empty directory; model ID recorded at run time), and Google Gemini (gemini-2.5-pro with gemini-2.5-flash fallback; free API; fallback cells counted and reported separately). Mistral (mistral-large-latest, packet cap 100k) is an optional fourth family. Claude Opus is not a coder. **Temperature:** the Claude and Codex CLIs expose no temperature and do not guarantee temperature 0, so those two coders are probably sampled; the Gemini and Mistral calls use temperature 0. Then verbatim quote verification and resolution. BM25 retrieval and exhaustive tagging are reported only as an ablation (dev-set recall 34-53%).
**Resolution rule:** the final level is the highest s that coders from at least two distinct families assign at s or above, each with verified quotes. Otherwise the cell is "not established" and scores 0. NA requires coders from at least two families. Reported: the share of cells resolved by fallback, and a majority-rule sensitivity analysis.
**Agreement:** ordinal Krippendorff's alpha per item and overall; NA counts as missing; bootstrap CIs over benchmarks. All coder pairs are cross-family under v3, so pairwise values are primary; the all-three figure is secondary.
**Contradiction flags (G2):** reported only after a check against the benchmark's own documentation and a right-of-reply offer. Flag verification involves a reader; the paper states who performed it and reports it apart from the scores.

## 5. Validation without human coders
**Perturbation suite:** for each item, packet variants of five types. Absolute sensitivity (expected level 2 whatever the base): easy injection, buried (late S1 appendix chunk only), paraphrase. Change detection, **relative to the base result by intent** (decoy and deletion labels depend on what the coders returned for the unperturbed packet, so they are not independent of the coders and are reported as change detection, not as specificity against ground truth): deletion (expected level 0; secondary label: the level fell below the base level) and decoy near-miss. Design: 40 multiplexed variant packets (benchmarks drawn by seed 20261004 from the 45). Within each variant every item receives exactly one perturbation type, by a balanced assignment, so each item x type pair occurs 8 times (1,000 labelled cells). Each variant is scored once with the full 25-item call (40 calls per coder). Decoys come in two halves: near-miss text in S1, and the full level-2 text placed only in a non-agent-visible code comment. Both halves expect at most the base level, because code comments do not count as reporting (coding manual G1a). Cells whose base result makes the label uninformative are dropped and counted, never replaced; A5 and A6 have 4 decoy cells instead of 8. **Authorship:** inject texts are canonical; paraphrase and decoy texts are re-authored before the freeze by a non-coder family (Groq qwen/qwen3.8-27b), and sensitivity is reported with that provenance stated. Cross-item interference is reported as a limitation. Reported: absolute sensitivity (inject, buried, paraphrase) and change-detection rate (deletion, decoy) per item and per variant type, with Wilson 95% CIs, for each coder and for the resolved pipeline. Described as recovery of planted text and detection of change, not as ground truth.
**Published-human-gold check (no new human coding):** the closest automated-coding work validates against human labels (Rystrom 2601.20617; Kunilovskaya 2606.02255). We use human scores that are already published.
(a) ABC (2507.02825, App. D) gives binary expert assessments of 10 agentic benchmarks. The pipeline scores those benchmarks on items T.1-T.9 and R.1-R.13 (220 cells; T.10 excluded because the instrument and the published assessment define different constructs), with the item text adapted only to ABC's binary scale.
(b) BetterBench (2411.12990) released per-criterion expert scores for 24 benchmarks, of which 23 are obtainable (MMMU is missing from the released data) (CC BY 4.0). The pipeline scores them on BetterBench's own items, using a seeded sample of 20 of 46 criteria (seed 20261004) x 23 benchmarks (460 cells).
Reported: agreement with the published scores (Cohen's kappa or weighted kappa, raw agreement), per coder and resolved. Caveat: those scores refer to the benchmark versions available when ABC and BetterBench scored them, so we pin the nearest version by date. **There is no expert gold for the agent items A1-A11.** This is stated in the paper; A-items rest on perturbation, cross-family agreement and right of reply.
**External check:** maintainer right of reply on their own scores. Reported: the response rate, and corrections in each direction (score raised or lowered), with the evidence surface each correction cites; scores are shown before and after correction. It is presented as an external check only if its window closes before submission.

## 6. Executed scoring
As in `research/executed/plan-v1.md` (**run design v3**, 2026-10-04, before any full run; supersedes v2): the selection rule with its amendments, then 3 benchmarks (AgentClinic and RadA-BenchPlat pass criterion (v); Synthetic Hospital passed (v) under v3, so no backup was needed); agent qwen3:4b-instruct-2507-q8_0 (derived tag qwen3-4b-ctx16k); simulator and judge llama3.1:8b-instruct-q4_K_M at temperature 0 (derived tag llama3.1-8b-ctx16k), all on ollama and pinned by digest; the judge never equals the agent. Two conditions: A, agent at the benchmark's temperature (AgentClinic 0.05; others 0); B, agent at 0.7. 10 seeded tasks (seed 20261004) x 5 repeats per condition = 100 episodes per benchmark, 300 in total. CP-Env was dropped (live-web drift; a hosted model rejected a request) and MedSP1000 was dropped (no episode completed); the change from five benchmarks to three is reported as a deviation. Operational metrics: action divergence, verdict instability, pass^k with cluster bootstrap CIs (10 clusters per benchmark, so wide), CI width at 1 versus 5 runs, and grader-versus-state disagreement (Synthetic Hospital). Results are per-benchmark, never population rates. No MedAgentBench (v1 or v2), tau2-bench, AgentDojo or MM-ToolSandbox execution. No benchmark data redistributed.

## 7. Analysis
RQ1: per-item share REPORTED (score of 1 or more) and fully reported (2), with Wilson 95% CIs, from the resolved scores. RQ2 and RQ3 as above. No confirmatory hypothesis tests.

**Analyses added after design review 1 (adjudicated 2026-10-05; each is a plan, to be run after the freeze):**
1. **Coder test-retest** (`agentaudit retest`). Each coder re-scores a seeded 10 of the 45 packets (seed 20261004; the 10 packets with the smallest hash of `seed|retest|id`), with the same packet, cap and prompt. Reported per coder: raw agreement and ordinal alpha between its first and second scores (bootstrap CI over the 10 benchmarks), weighted kappa, and agreement of the resolved levels. Purpose: measure self-consistency of coders that are probably sampled.
2. **Judge-only rerun** (`analysis/judge_rerun.py`). For AgentClinic, the moderator's decision is re-judged on the recorded transcripts 3 times with the v3 judge model (the recorded moderator prompt, temperature 0; agent and simulator not rerun). Reported: the share of transcripts whose re-judgements are not all identical and the share that differ from the recorded verdict, with Wilson 95% CIs. Purpose: separate judge variance from end-to-end variance. Requires ollama.
3. **R-rule sensitivity** (`analysis/rq1_rq2.py --exclude-rule-affected`). RQ1 prevalence and the alpha table are recomputed after excluding every benchmark whose inclusion depends on R1-R7 or R6a (frozen-list rows whose `rule_applied` names such a rule, or whose adjudicated decision differs from the first-pass decision and is INCLUDE; `research/eligibility/adjudication.md`). Outputs carry the suffix `_ruleexcl`; the primary results keep all 45.
4. **Screening-recall check.** A seeded 300 of the records that Claude Sonnet excluded at title-and-abstract screening are re-screened by qwen/qwen3.8-27b on Groq (a different family). Reported: the share the second screener would send to full text, with a Wilson 95% interval; every such record is assessed against E1-E5.
